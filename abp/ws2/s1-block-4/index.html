Block 4 · Challenge: all five test mails right

On this page

Your Triage flow sorts some of the five test mails wrong. A ready workflow gives it a score, three rounds per click. Then you improve the prompt until all five are right.

Test mailSubjectExpected
8Papersreply
30(no subject)forward
46Market Forecasting Conferencereply
67RE: Jumparchive
126Contact Info for Conference Call Tomorrow at 10 am EDTcalendar

  1. Import n8n/ws2_1_test_triage_improved.json from the folder n8n. It gives WS2.1 - TEST - Triage - Improved.

  2. Check its credential: double-click the node fetch_flow. Under Header Auth, it should say Langflow (Run API). If not, pick it. Close the node, then save.

  3. Before you run it, look at its nodes. Double-click each one:

    NodeWhat it does
    trigger_manualstarts it when you click Execute workflow
    webhook_open_runsstarts it from its page in your browser: opening the page shows the dashboard, its button runs the test
    set_test_settingsthe settings: rounds (3), the flow (enron-triage, your Triage flow), and the field of its answer to compare (action)
    set_settings_from_page, if_run_requestedwhat the page’s form sent, over the settings; a test runs only after a click on Run the test, or Execute workflow
    get_casesthe five test mails from Cases, as in your WS2.1
    transform_into_roundseach test mail once per round: 15 items
    fetch_flowthe 15 mails to your Triage flow, five at a time
    set_answerTriage’s answer as fields, as in your WS2.1
    set_scoreeach action next to the expected one: the score of each round, the seconds, every answer
    post_run_to_runsone line into Runs
    get_runs, transform_runs_into_dashboardevery line of Runs as a dashboard
    format_dashboard_as_html, send_dashboard_pagethe dashboard in n8n, and as a page in your browser

    Check yourself: your WS2.1 writes each action into Cases. Does this workflow do that too?

    The answer

    No. The one node that writes, post_run_to_runs, writes into Runs. Cases holds the test: the five mails and what is expected. A test should not change its test set. The results go into Runs, one line per click, so you can compare one prompt with the next.

    And: why does it send each mail several times?

    The answer

    The model does not always give the same answer to the same mail. One round can be lucky, or unlucky. Three rounds show whether a prompt holds. Want more? Set more rounds: a clearer picture, a longer wait.

  4. Run it once here, in n8n:

    1. Click the small arrow next to Execute workflow and pick trigger_manual.
    2. Click Execute workflow.
    3. When all nodes are green, double-click the node format_dashboard_as_html. On the right: your dashboard.

    Why pick a trigger? With two triggers, n8n starts from the webhook unless you pick another. The webhook would wait for a call of its page, and nothing happens. n8n picks the webhook again after each reload.

  5. Open the dashboard as a page, to keep next to Langflow:

    1. Click Publish at the top, then Publish again in the window. The page works only while the workflow is published.

    2. Open this address in a new browser tab:

      http://localhost:5678/webhook/triage-runs

    3. At the top, the form: what you changed, the rounds, the flow, the field. Its button Run the test runs the test. The page comes back when all rounds are done, with the new run.

  6. Read the dashboard:

    • The most recent run: right answers per round, on average. Is it better than the run before, and than your start? Only beyond the spread counts: every round better than every round before.
    • Every run: one row per run, one dot per round. The spread of the dots is the model’s luck.
    • Every test mail, run by run: in how many rounds each mail was right. ▲ or ▼: better or worse than the run before. The mails that work worst come first.
    • Wrong in the most recent run: what the model answered.
    • Each run in detail: click a run, and see every answer of every round.

  7. In Cases, read the mails it got wrong, in the column mail. Why would the model choose that?

  8. In Langflow, open Triage (Enron), then the node Prompt Template. Change how the actions are described. Only the prompt. Save.

  9. On the page, write what you changed, and click Run the test. Did your fix for one mail break another? The table of test mails shows it.

Done: you test your Triage flow the right way: three rounds per click, one line per run, the test set untouched, and a dashboard that shows what each change did.

Our prompt, for after the challenge: 5 of 5, ten times in a row

In Langflow, open Triage (Enron), then the node Prompt Template. Click into Template, select all of it, and paste this in its place. Save the flow, then run WS2.1 - TEST - Triage - Improved again.

You sort the incoming mail of Vince J Kaminski, head of the research group at Enron in Houston, in 2001.
Read the mail below and answer with one JSON object and nothing else: no explanation, no code fence.

The object has four keys:
  "events"    a list of strings: the names of every MailEx event in the mail's own text that is addressed to
              Kaminski or to all recipients, for example ["Request_Data", "Deliver_Data"]; [] if none. The names:
  Request_Data           asks for information
  Request_Action         asks someone to do something
  Request_Meeting        asks for a meeting or call
  Request_Action_Data    asks about an action (has it been done, when will it be)
  Request_Meeting_Data   asks about a meeting (time, place, who comes)
  Deliver_Data           gives information
  Deliver_Action_Data    says an action is done, will be done, or will not be
  Deliver_Meeting_Data   gives a meeting's time, place, agenda or outcome
  Amend_Data, Amend_Action_Data, Amend_Meeting_Data   corrects or changes something given before
  "action"    what his assistant does with the mail, one of:
                "calendar"  a meeting, call, visit or interview is asked for or set, or how it will take place
                            (time, place, who calls whom): to Shirley Crenshaw (his assistant) or into the calendar
                "reply"     someone wants information, an answer or a decision from Kaminski himself: draft a reply in
                            his name
                "forward"   someone wants work done that his assistant or his team can do: send documents or slides,
                            make copies, fill in a form, book, register; forward it to whoever does it, or note it as
                            a task
                "archive"   it only delivers, informs or announces (reports, newsletters, FYI, thanks, spam): file it
              Pick the one that matters most if several apply. Work to be done by a date matters more than a
              request to confirm receipt.
  "deadline"  a date or time by which something is wanted, as written in the mail; "" if none
  "why"       one short sentence

The mail:
{mail}

What changed: reply only for what Kaminski himself must answer; forward for work his assistant or his team can do; calendar also for how a call will take place; and work to be done by a date matters more than a request to confirm receipt.

Five mails are not enough to know it is better overall. On all 140 test mails of the case, it got about as many right as the prompt as it comes.

Test your project’s Langflow flows from n8n, too

The same works for every Langflow flow of your team’s project: a copy of WS2.1 - TEST - Triage - Improved, with your test cases and your flow. You see every answer, and you can run the test again after each change.

It also avoids the Langflow 1.8 memory bug (what it is). In the Playground, chats with the same name share one memory across flows, so a test can see another flow’s chat. A call from n8n never does: each call of the workflow has its own session id, so a flow with memory starts fresh for every case.

  1. Write your test cases. Open ws2_1_test_triage_cases.csv in Excel or Numbers and keep its first line, the names of the columns. Below it, one test case per line:

    • test_mail: a number for the case
    • subject: a short title
    • mail: what your flow gets, the text you would type into its Playground
    • expected: what it should answer
    • action: leave it empty

    Save it as CSV, with a new name.

  2. Make two data tables, as in block 2, with your team’s name and what you test, for example:

    • Blue - TEST - Intake - Cases, from your new file
    • Blue - TEST - Intake - Runs, from ws2_1_test_triage_runs.csv

  3. Open WS2.1 - TEST - Triage - Improved. Open the menu … at the top, next to its name, then Duplicate. Name the copy after your test, for example Blue - TEST - Intake.

  4. In the copy, pick your tables: double-click get_cases and pick your Cases table in the field Data table. Do the same in post_run_to_runs and get_runs, with your Runs table.

  5. Give the page an address of its own: double-click webhook_open_runs and change Path, for example to blue-intake-runs. Two published workflows cannot share a path.

  6. Double-click set_test_settings and change the values:

    • flow_endpoint: your flow. In Langflow, open it, click Share at the top right, then API access. Copy what comes after /run/ in the address, up to ? if there is one.
    • answer_field: does your flow answer with JSON, as Triage does? Then the field to compare with expected, for example action. Does it answer with text? Then leave it empty: a case counts as right when the answer contains expected.
    • rounds: how often each case goes to the flow.

    The page’s form can change all three for one run.

  7. Click Publish. Open your page, for example http://localhost:5678/webhook/blue-intake-runs, and click Run the test.
