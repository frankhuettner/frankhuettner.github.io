Agentic AI in Business · Workshop II

Homework until 23 Nov: get more out of Claude Code

On this page
  • Your goals for this homework
  • Before you start
  • Your terminal, in short

Why this homework? Working with AI feels fast. But feeling fast is not the same as being fast. In 2025, the research group METR measured it: 16 experienced developers worked on 246 real tasks, some with AI tools and some without. With AI, the developers believed that they were 20% faster. The clock said: they were 19% slower (METR, 2025).

The tools are better today, and METR itself says that this number is out of date (METR, 2026). But one lesson stays: you cannot feel whether the AI makes you faster. So do not trust the feeling: learn how to work with the tool. This homework shows you how, introducing nine habits that you’ll try out yourself.

You do not have everything from the course on your laptop? Maybe you are not in the course. Maybe you have n8n and Langflow, but you did not do the last homework. Or you just want to work on a project of your own. You can do this homework too: wherever you see a box or a line New here?, always open it, and do what it says.

Your goals for this homework

  • You know which LLM works inside Claude Code, what that changes for you, and how you can change it. An LLM (large language model) is an AI that reads and writes text; ChatGPT runs on one, too. From here on, we call it “the model”.

  • Claude Code knows the facts of your setup (here: your kingo-pod, the course software on your laptop), so it does not have to guess.

  • You know nine habits that get you good results from Claude Code, in any project.

  • You planned and built a small website with Claude Code, in a folder of its own, outside the course.

  • You start Claude Code with one short command, from any folder.

  • Optional: you give Claude Code one more folder, you work in two sessions at once, and you know how to give a project a key of its own.

In the course, there is nothing new to install: you only update kingo-pod.

Where to type it. A sign before a command tells you where it goes:

  • a dollar sign: into your terminal (on Windows: the Ubuntu app), as in ./kingo status;
  • sparkles: into Claude Code, where you write to the model, as in /clear.

The sign is not part of the command: do not type it. In a box, the Copy button leaves it out.

Before you start

  • Deadline: Monday, 23 November, before the lecture at 10:50.
  • You need, in the course:

    • your kingo-pod, updated and running. Paste this line into your terminal: it updates kingo-pod and starts it.

      cd ~/kingo-pod && ./kingo update

      Near the end, it says Stack updated — kingo-pod and the version: 1.4.28 or higher, or 1.5.27 or higher with Kingo 1.5.

    • Claude Code, set up as in the last homework: installed with its sandbox (step 2), with a key in n8n (step 3), and started once with ./kingo claude, where it took your OpenRouter key and your n8n key (step 4);

    • your team’s project: the Langflow flow and the n8n workflow you work on.

  • New here: without the course, without the last homework, or for a project of your own? You can do this homework too, and you need less: open the two boxes below.
New here? What you need, and your first start

You need no n8n, no Langflow and no Podman: Claude Code works in a folder of your own. You need four things:

  1. A terminal. On a Mac: the app Terminal. On Windows: the Ubuntu app, a Linux that runs inside Windows. To install it, follow steps 1 and 2 of kingo-pod’s guide for Windows. The steps after them install n8n and Langflow, which you do not need.

  2. kingo-pod. This line copies kingo-pod into your home folder. On a Mac, paste it into Terminal:

    git clone https://github.com/frankhuettner/kingo-pod.git ~/kingo-pod

    A window asks to install the “Command Line Developer Tools”? Click Install, wait until it is done, and paste the line again. On Windows, paste this line into the Ubuntu app. It asks for your Linux password, which you do not see as you type:

    sudo apt update && sudo apt install -y git python3 && git clone https://github.com/frankhuettner/kingo-pod.git ~/kingo-pod

  3. Claude Code. Paste this line, on a Mac and in Ubuntu:

    curl -fsSL https://claude.ai/install.sh | bash

  4. An OpenRouter key of your own. OpenRouter passes what Claude Code sends on to the model, and bills it to your key. On openrouter.ai, make an account and add credits. Then open Settings, then Keys, make a key, and give it a credit limit: the key stops at that amount.

Your first start. Make a folder to practise in, and start Claude Code there. Paste:

mkdir -p ~/projects/practice && cd ~/kingo-pod && ./kingo claude --in ~/projects/practice

The first time, kingo gets nono, the sandbox that Claude Code works in. Then it asks for your OpenRouter key: paste it, and press Enter. You do not see the key as you paste it: that is on purpose. Then kingo gets a browser, with which Claude Code looks at the pages that it writes. In Ubuntu, kingo may ask to install a few packages for the browser: press Enter, and type your Linux password.

kingo asks for nothing else here: no key of n8n, and no mailbox. Both belong to the setup of the course, and you can skip them for this homework:

  • A key of n8n. Only the start for the course asks for it: ./kingo claude without --in. That start also needs n8n and Langflow running.
  • A mailbox. No start of Claude Code asks for it. It is for the mail AI assistant of the course, and only the command ./kingo mail sets it up.

You can do both later, whenever you like: your practice folder and your OpenRouter key stay as they are. The pages of the course show how. kingo-pod’s guide installs n8n and Langflow, the preparatory homework sets up the mailbox, and steps 3 and 4 of the last homework show the first start for the course.

You also have a Claude Pro or Max subscription? Then you can let it pay for the model. Start with --login:

cd ~/kingo-pod && ./kingo claude --login --in ~/projects/practice

Then you log in to Claude, once: Use your Claude subscription shows how.

Claude Code then asks you to pick a theme, and whether you trust the files in this folder: choose Yes, it is your own folder. Type /exit to leave.

How you read this homework. It speaks to the students of the course: of their team’s project, and of their last homework. Where you do something else, a box or a line New here? says what. In every Your turn, unless it says otherwise:

  • where you start Claude Code with ./kingo claude, start it with ./kingo claude --in ~/projects/practice;
  • where it says your course folder, ~/kingo-pod/claude, take your folder ~/projects/practice.

From Anthropic’s Advanced setup, OpenRouter’s FAQ and its page on limits, and kingo-pod’s guides for Mac and Windows.

Stuck? Help for new readers

New here? The words of the course
  • kingo-pod: the course software on your laptop. In the course, n8n and Langflow run in it. Its command, kingo, also starts Claude Code in a sandbox: ./kingo claude, when you are in the folder ~/kingo-pod.
  • The sandbox: a fence around Claude Code, here nono. Inside the fence, Claude Code works as it likes. Outside, it can read nothing and change nothing.
  • The mail AI assistant: what the teams build in the course. It reads new mail, sorts it, and drafts answers, with the help of older mails in a database. It sends nothing without your OK.
  • Langflow and n8n: the two tools in which the teams build it. Langflow holds the flows, the brain of the assistant: for example the flow that drafts an answer. n8n holds the workflows, its muscle: they fetch the mail, call the flows, and send the answers that you approve.

Your terminal, in short

More than one terminal. As in a browser, you can open more than one tab: one for Claude Code, one for your own commands.

  • On a Mac, in Terminal: press Cmd+T for a new tab.
  • On Windows, in the Ubuntu window: click the small arrow ˅ next to the + at the top, and choose Ubuntu. The + alone may open PowerShell, which runs commands in Windows, not in Ubuntu. No + at the top? Start the Ubuntu app a second time: that gives you a second window.

Where am I? ~ is your home folder: ~/kingo-pod is the folder kingo-pod in it. These commands help you find your way:

  • pwd shows the folder that the terminal is in.
  • ls lists what is in that folder. With a folder after it, it lists that folder: ls ~/kingo-pod shows what is in your kingo-pod.
  • cd with a folder goes into that folder. cd alone goes back to your home folder.
  • clear empties the window of the terminal.

From Apple’s Open new Terminal windows and tabs and Microsoft’s Windows Terminal.
