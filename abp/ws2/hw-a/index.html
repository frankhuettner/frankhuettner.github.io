Homework, Part A: Set up your test mailbox

Steps 1 to 7: update kingo-pod, make the account, copy Kaminski's mails into it.

1. Update kingo-pod: get the new part called ‘Mail’

kingo-pod is the course software on your laptop. Langflow and n8n run in it. This update adds a new part called Mail. It connects your test Gmail account to kingo-pod. Later, it also sends the answers you approve.

Open a terminal (on a Mac: the Terminal app; on Windows: the Ubuntu app) and run this one line:

cd ~/kingo-pod && git checkout -- . && git pull --ff-only && ./kingo backup && ./kingo update && ./kingo mode abp

Near the end, you should see a line that starts with Stack updated — kingo-pod 1.4.3 (or a higher number).

This can take a few minutes. You don’t have to wait: go on with steps 2 to 5 in your browser. The update keeps running in the terminal.

In case you care about what this does
  • && just connects the commands: each one starts when the one before it worked. If one fails, the line stops there.
  • cd ~/kingo-pod goes to your kingo-pod folder.
  • git checkout -- . undoes changes in your kingo-pod folder. For Workshop I, many of you added nemoguardrails to langflow/requirements.txt. That change stopped every update since. kingo-pod now has nemoguardrails built in.
  • git pull --ff-only gets the newest version.
  • ./kingo backup saves your Langflow flows and n8n workflows first.
  • ./kingo update builds the new version.
  • ./kingo mode abp starts the parts we need: Langflow, n8n, the PostgreSQL database, CloudBeaver and a service called Mail.

Stuck? Help for step 1

2. Make your test Gmail account

  1. Go to https://accounts.google.com/signup. Google will ask for your phone number.
  2. Use this pattern for the address: n8n.<givenname>.<familyname>@gmail.com
    • For example, for Min-ji Kim: n8n.minji.kim@gmail.com (small letters, no spaces, no hyphens)
    • If the address is taken, add a 2: n8n.minji.kim2@gmail.com
  3. When Google suggests addresses, click Create your own Gmail address. Type the part before @gmail.com.

This account is only for the course. Do not use your private account or your SKKU account: the AI assistant you build will read and send mail in this account.

3. Set your test Gmail account to English

In Gmail, signed in with your test Gmail account: gear icon → See all settings → General → Language: English (US) → Save Changes (at the bottom of the page). Do this before step 6: then the folders in your test Gmail account have English names, as in this guide.

4. Turn on 2-Step Verification (needed for step 5)

Go to https://myaccount.google.com/security → 2-Step Verification → turn it on. Choose text messages, the Google prompt, or an authenticator app. A security key alone does not work for step 5.

5. Make an app password for kingo-pod

  1. Go to https://myaccount.google.com/apppasswords.
  2. Type a name for it, for example kingo mail, and click Create. The name does not matter; it only helps you find the password on this page later.
  3. Google shows 16 letters, only once. Copy them.

This is not your Google password. It is a second password, only for kingo-pod: with it, kingo-pod can read your test Gmail account and send the answers you approve. You can delete it any time on the same page.

Stuck? Help for step 5

6. Connect your test Gmail account to kingo-pod

First check that step 1 is done. In the terminal, in your kingo-pod folder, run:

./kingo status

You should see Mail in the list. Then run:

./kingo mail

It asks three questions:

  1. The address of your test Gmail account. Type the full address, with @gmail.com, for example n8n.minji.kim@gmail.com.

  2. The app password from step 5. You will not see it while you type.

  3. Other addresses kingo-pod may send mail to. Type your SKKU address. On 9 November you will write from your SKKU address, and your AI assistant’s answers go back to it.

    Why kingo-pod asks for these addresses

    kingo-pod sends mail only to your test Gmail account and the addresses you type here. A mail to someone in Kaminski’s mails lands in your own test Gmail account instead. A mail to any other address is not sent at all. So neither your AI assistant nor a mistake can send mail to anyone else.

    Forgot your SKKU address, or want to add another one later? Run ./kingo mail again. Press Enter to keep your test Gmail account, paste the app password again (or make a new one as in step 5), then type the addresses.

kingo-pod then checks the login. It sends nothing. You should see a line that starts with OK. Folders:.

Stuck? Help for step 6

7. Copy Kaminski’s mails into your test Gmail account

Your AI assistant will search Kaminski’s old mails for facts when it drafts an answer. The update in step 1 brought all 3,244 mails onto your laptop, inside kingo-pod. Now you copy them from kingo-pod into your test Gmail account.

  1. In the terminal, in your kingo-pod folder, run:

    ./kingo mail seed

    After a short while, you see a line that starts with Mailbox: and ends with Uploading starts now.

    What happens

    In a few seconds, the command puts all mails into kingo-pod’s service called Mail, on your laptop. Mail keeps this copy: with it, Mail knows what is new on each side. From there, kingo-pod copies the mails into its PostgreSQL database and uploads them to your test Gmail account, round by round. In the end, every mail is in three places: in Gmail, in Mail, and in PostgreSQL, where n8n and your AI assistant read it.

  2. The command is done, but the upload goes on inside kingo-pod for about 30 minutes, sometimes longer. Be patient. Keep kingo-pod running and your laptop awake.

  3. Watch the mails arrive. They come step by step over the 30 minutes, so the numbers in Gmail grow while you watch. Open Gmail in your browser, signed in with your test Gmail account. When the upload is done, you see:

    • Sent (in the menu on the left): the mails Kaminski wrote, about 870 conversations.
    • All Mail (in the menu on the left, under More): about 1,600 conversations.
    • Inbox: stays empty for now. That is right: new mail comes in step 12 and in the workshop.
    Why not 3,244?

    Gmail counts conversations, not mails: a mail and all its answers are one conversation. So Gmail shows smaller numbers, but all 3,244 mails are there.

    To count the mails, ask kingo-pod: run ./kingo mail status. It shows each folder twice, here and at Gmail. When the numbers are the same in every row, the upload is done. (No such rows? Run ./kingo update first.)

  4. You don’t have to wait for the upload: go on with Part B now. The upload keeps running in the background.

Good to know:

  • ./kingo mail seed copies every mail only once. If you run it again, kingo-pod answers already seeded and gives the upload a push.
  • If your laptop sleeps or kingo-pod stops, start it again with ./kingo up. The upload goes on by itself.
